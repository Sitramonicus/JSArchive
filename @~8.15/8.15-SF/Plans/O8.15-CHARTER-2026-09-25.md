# O8.15 — premise and the 8.14 residual ledger (2026-09-25)

**Status:** charter. Nothing built, nothing changed in the frozen pack. This is the document that says what
8.15 is *for* and what came in with it.

---

## 1. Premise

8.14 shipped and the decoder fails on it — that standing is kept and is not to be traded away. 8.15 exists to
close **one known, exploitable weakness** in the way the pack is assembled, without giving up anything 8.14
achieved.

The weakness is **linear/series stitching**: the 23 pieces are laid out one after another, so a piece is a
contiguous byte range, and the payload's chunk order is *published in the runner* as a 98 KB permutation
table. Neither of those needs an analyser to defeat — a script does it.

**Why that matters even though the other team knows the structure** (operator, 2026-09-25):

> "If I just sit idly by knowing the weakness of our system is exploitable then we're not really progressing.
> The point of there being another team despite knowing the general structure of the system is to test their
> decoders as well — a tug and pull on who can implement better plating and supports which makes it an
> advancing dynamic that helps each other out. The current linear/series stitching we were doing is a flaw I
> intend to get fixed."

Read plainly: the objective is **not secrecy** — the structure is shared knowledge and the repo is public on
purpose. The objective is **cost**. Today, splitting the bundle into pieces costs nothing. After 8.15 it
should cost real work: parse, classify, build a dependency graph, understand the code. That is what keeps the
tug-of-war productive on both sides, and it is the reason the weave is primary work rather than optional
hardening.

### The law that governs every step

**The supplement must not be a regression.** A step is promoted only if, on the exact built bytes:

1. the whole battery returns **identical verdicts to the frozen 8.14 pack** — same numbers, not just "no new
   failures" (`chain-revive-check`, `hold-check`, `gate-replay --spec`, matrix, tiers, carrier-flip, decoy
   parity, dangling-refs, leakcensus, golden trace);
2. `detector-replay` still passes and the stand-down behaviour is unchanged (silence unclaimed, garden
   fallback, teardown) — that is the product, and it gets its own line in the acceptance table;
3. 8.14 and the b22 rollback stay untouched and recoverable;
4. nothing ships that cannot be **shown** behaviour-neutral. "Probably fine" is exactly the regression the
   operator ruled out.

## 2. The two arms of the work

| arm | what it fixes | the measured starting point |
|---|---|---|
| **A — the published map** | The runner ships `o0…o3`: a permutation of chunk indices 0…18184 (**verified unique, 98 KB**). The payload's original layout is readable off the file | 18,185 chunks, ~176 chars each, dealt into 4 groups; the loader needs the order *only* because it is stored |
| **B — linear stitching** | 23 pieces laid end to end; one statement is **211 KB** and 18 statements are ≥50 KB; pieces are recoverable as regions | 143 statements in one scope; 30 binding-level dependency edges; longest forced chain **3**; 43 hoisted declarations (20.4% of bytes) **proven** relocatable with identical behaviour |

Work order: **A alone → 2 (split) → 3 (weave) → 4 (constraint pass) → 5 (build identity)**. A is
independently verifiable and ships first; 2 must pass the golden trace on its own before 3 goes on top.
Texture normalisation (old step 6) is **dropped** — the shipped bundle already reads as ordinary minified JS.
