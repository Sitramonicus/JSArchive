## 3. Arm A — the published map is gone

What it was: the runner embeds its own copy of the cover as 18,185 shuffled base64 chunks — and shipped the
shuffle **as plain JSON** (`o0…o3`, ~98 KB). An analyst could rebuild the embedded cover by reading the
file: no decoder, no analysis, just an inverse permutation.

What it is now: the chunk deal is **regenerated at load time** from a 32-bit seed (the loader gains ~10 lines
of mulberry32 + Fisher–Yates; old fragments still decode). A build-time guard regenerates the deal exactly
as the loader will and fails the build on any mismatch.

Evidence: the arm A runner's embedded snapshot still reassembles to the frozen cover digest `3bf21868…`, the
payload it stages is **byte-identical** to 8.14's (`28d36d97…`), and the runner is 96,939 B smaller. Battery
on the arm A runner: matrix 26/0, tiers 42/0, hold-check PASS, carrier-flip 14/0, repaste 5/5.

**Honest framing:** this is cost, not secrecy. The seed and the generator are in the artifact; what is gone
is the *free* inverse permutation. That is the tug-and-pull you described.

## 4. Step 2 — the split: written, measured, and one correction to the plan

The transform: `var A=[e0…en]` → `var A=[];A.push(…);A.push(…);` — runs execute in the original position, so
values, order and timing are unchanged; only the shape changes. It re-parses its own output and refuses to
write unless the literal sequence is identical.

Measured on the shipped payload: **12 arrays, 356 KB of array mass, +1,437 chars (0.07 %)**, 8 statements
fall below the 15 KB line, and every payload gate is unchanged.

**One correction worth your attention:** the roadmap assumed the big units were flat literals. They are not.
The largest interior statements (70–92 KB) are `var X = (function(){…})()` — an IIFE bound to a name. A
statement like that cannot be "split into runs"; but its **body is already a grid of statements**, so the
weave does not need a split there — it needs to work *inside* function bodies. So step 3's unit census must
be taken **per function body**, not per file. That is a real change to how the weave is built, and it is
cheaper than the plan assumed (no split needed for the IIFE mass).
