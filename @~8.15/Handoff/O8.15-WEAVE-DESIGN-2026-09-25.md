# O8.15 — weave design: making the pieces un-separable (2026-09-25)

**Status:** design + measured feasibility, with a **working prototype of the core primitive**. No frozen
bytes changed, nothing rebuilt — the 8.14 pack (`Working-Stable/O8.14/FROZEN-2026-09-22.md`) is untouched.
Prototype tool: `tools/weave-probe.mjs` (not wired into the board).

**The ask:** stop the pieces from being merely *stitched in parallel*, and impose a weave that (a) makes a
piece's code not recoverable as a contiguous thing, and (b) can't be un-woven by reading the file.

---

## 1. Why the current arrangement is un-weavable in principle

The assembly is **four top-level calls**, one of which carries 2,430,297 of 2,433,594 bytes (99.9%). Inside
that one scope:

| what | count | bytes | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `var` declarations (data tables, handles, big blobs) | 31 | 1,085,081 | 44.6% |
| expression statements (27 of them are IIFE calls) | 51 | 848,920 | 34.9% |
| **function declarations** | 43 | 496,203 | 20.4% |
| try / empty statements | 18 | 83 | 0.0% |
| **total** | **143** | **2,430,287** | |

So the file is 143 statements in one flat scope. Today those 143 statements sit in **piece order**, which is
why a piece is a contiguous byte range. Nothing about that is inherent — it is an assembly choice.

## 2. The primitive: hoisting is a free relocation

A `function` declaration at the top of a scope is installed **before any statement in that scope runs**.
Its position therefore carries no meaning whatsoever. That is the one thing in this file that can be moved
arbitrarily, with no runtime cost, no inverse, and no loader support.

The prototype exploits exactly that: keep the order-keeping spine in relative order, deal the 43 hoistable
units into seeded gaps around it, then **splice the original text spans** — nothing is reprinted, so no
minifier or printer can introduce a semantic drift. The only added bytes are `;` separators (142 of them).

**Evidence (run 2026-09-25 on the frozen bundle):**

| check | result |
|---|---|
| units relocated | 43 of 143 (**20.4%** of the scope's bytes) |
| units pinned (duplicate/shadowing names) | **0** — no name collisions in this build |
| `node --check` on the woven output | OK |
| behaviour, seeds 1/2/3 (`tools/fullwire-probe.mjs`, timestamps normalised) | **byte-identical output to the original**, all three seeds: ran clean, `GoogleUblock` + `GoogleVault` published, wrong-passphrase call still answers `false` |
| size cost | +142 bytes |

## 3. How far a real weave can go (measured, not guessed)

Binding-level dependency analysis of those same 143 units (a name bound by `var`/`let`/`const` in the scope,
used by a unit that executes at load):

| metric | value |
|---|---:|
| dependency edges | **30** |
| longest forced chain | **3 units** |
| units with no binding-level ordering constraint | **111 of 143** |
| bytes in that free set | 1,605,414 of 2,430,287 — **66.1%** |

**The "spine" is not a spine.** Two thirds of the file has no binding-level reason to be in any particular
order, and the deepest forced chain is three units long. A weave that respects the dependency graph can
therefore relocate ~2/3 of the bytes and reorder the rest within wide freedom, while the four top-level
calls and the chain handshake keep working.

**The honest caveat:** that 66.1% is an *upper bound*. It measures binding-level constraints only —
**property-level** effects are invisible to it (`S._0x5 = ...` in one unit, read in another), as are timer
ordering and the boot handshake. Those must be caught by execution, which is why the acceptance test is
K seeds × the golden trace, not an argument.

## 4. What makes a weave *un-untanglable* — and what currently isn't

Three things currently give the arrangement away, in increasing order of severity:

1. **A stored inverse.** The carrier-level weave deals the payload's **18,185 chunks** into four interleaved
   groups — but the permutation is **written into the runner** as `o0…o3`: 98 KB of order tables, verified
   to be a full permutation of indices 0…18184. Anyone with the file can reconstruct the original order with
   a few lines of script. **The current weave ships its own map.**
2. **Texture clusters.** The pieces are distinguishable *inside* the bundle by obfuscator fingerprint, no
   markers needed. Measured across the 23 pieces: mean identifier length ranges **3.33 → 16.67**;
   `_0x`-prefixed identifiers appear in 9 pieces (up to 3,097 occurrences) and never in the others;
   zero-width-character encoding appears in 20 pieces (up to 427 chars) and not at all in 3. A change-point
   scan over those features recovers piece boundaries — a method that does not need any size difference to
   work.
3. **Size imbalance.** `shard-e1` at 440,656 B = 17.8% of the payload (see
   `Handoff/O8.15-SHARD-POPOUT-MEASUREMENT-2026-09-25.md`).

So the design has to answer all three, and the ordering matters: **weaving positions without normalising
texture just moves the fingerprint around.** The map from position to piece is destroyed, but the map from
*style* to piece is not.

### The design

**Stage 1 — texture normalisation (do this first).** A single uniform pass over the assembled bundle: one
identifier generator for the whole file, one string encoding, one numeric style, applied after stitching and
before the weave. This is what removes the clusters in §4.2. It replaces *per-piece* treatment diversity with
*global* uniformity, which is a real trade — a single deobfuscator that beats one region now beats all of
them — so this is the point to decide deliberately, not to drift into.

**Stage 2 — the weave (positions).** Unit-level, dependency-respecting, seeded:
- hoistable declarations → free relocation (proven, §2);
- everything else → topological order from the def-use graph, with the freedom in §3 consumed as shuffle;
- emitted by splicing original spans, never reprinting.

**Stage 3 — the property that makes it un-untanglable: the weave is never recorded.** There is no inverse,
because the runtime never needs one — the file only needs *an* order, not the original one. This is the
sharp difference from the carrier-level weave, which must carry `o0…o3` because the loader has to undo the
deal. A unit-level weave has nothing to undo: the mapping *unit → piece* exists nowhere in the shipped
bytes, and it cannot be recomputed from them, because the pieces are not in the file in a recoverable
grouping at all. Recovering a piece becomes a code-understanding exercise, not a table lookup.

**Corollary for the carrier (cheap win, worth doing regardless):** replace the stored `o0…o3` with a
permutation **derived** from the salt/seed the loader already computes (`slowChain(SALT ^ FNVn(名) ^ …)`,
the same state that drives the FY positions). That deletes ~98 KB of order tables and removes the printed
map. Honest limit: the derivation lives in the loader, so a determined analyst can still re-derive the
order — this raises the cost from *read a table* to *reimplement the PRF*. It is not cryptographic hardness.

**Stage 4 — keep our own map, privately.** A build-time artifact (never shipped) recording unit → piece, so
we can still attribute behaviour to a piece when debugging. The weave costs us ergonomics; this buys most of
it back without weakening the shipped property.

## 5. Verification plan

| gate | what it proves |
|---|---|
| **new `weave-equivalence.mjs`** | K seeds (say 12) × the golden trace: every weave must yield the identical 25-event sequence. This is the acceptance test for the whole idea — it is cheap (the golden run takes seconds) and it directly attacks the one thing the analysis cannot promise (property-level ordering) |
| existing battery | `chain-revive-check` 14/14 · `hold-check` 9/9 · `gate-replay --spec` 17/17 · `dangling-refs --catch-only` 0-on-error-path · decoy parity ×3 · golden 25 |
| **new locality metric** | per piece, the spread of its units' positions: assert no piece has more than X% of its units inside any 10%-wide window of the file. This is the measurable "a piece is no longer a region" criterion, and it is the number to put in the release notes |
| live paste | required, as always, because the venue responds to the boot order in ways a fixture does not model |

## 6. What this does and does not buy

**Does:** removes the free structural shortcut. Today, locating a piece is "find the byte range"; after a
normalised weave it is "parse, classify, build a def-use graph, then understand the code". It also removes
the change-point scan (§4.2) and the size tell (§4.3).

**Does not:** make the code harder to *read* once found — obfuscation quality is unchanged; protect against a
determined analyst who is willing to understand the payload; or add any cryptographic hardness. It should
not be described as encryption or as making recovery infeasible.

**Cost:** one new pipeline stage after `s4` and before `stego` (parser already a dependency; ~1 s per build);
+142 bytes; a new gate; and a permanent ergonomic tax that Stage 4 mitigates. **Risk:** it restructures the
shipped artifact, so it needs the full battery, the new gate and a live paste before it is trusted.

**Explicitly not proposed:** any self-integrity check or anti-tamper response in the payload. The redteam's
own note from the O.8.9 line is the rule we keep — anti-tamper raises reviewer suspicion and reads as a
tell. The weave should be silent.

## 7. Decision needed

1. **Stage 1 (texture normalisation)** — this is the one with a real trade (uniformity vs per-piece
   diversity). It is also the step that decides whether "indistinguishable" is meaningfully true.
2. **Stage 2–3 (the weave itself)** — build it as a new stage, or leave 8.14 as-is and carry the design?
3. **The carrier de-tabling** (derive `o0…o3`) — cheap, saves bytes, independent of the rest. Worth doing on
   its own if the weave is deferred.

All three are 8.15 work; none of it touches the frozen pack.
