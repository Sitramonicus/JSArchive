## What this means

- **The interaction is systematic, not unlucky.** `js-confuser` emits self-consistent code; our
  JSO-light pass over that output is what renames a binding and leaves its call sites behind. Ten
  independent rolls cannot be a coincidence, so no amount of "re-roll until green" fixes it.
- **The gate is doing its job.** `tools/dangling-refs.mjs` (`--catch-only`) is what surfaced this in
  every roll, offline, before any paste. That gate is now the reason this class can never ship silently.
- **Action taken:** `vm: false` restored in `oto/scripts/obf-mound-e.js`, with the campaign result
  written into the source comment so nobody re-attempts it without reading this first.
- **Cost of the decision:** e3 ships at ~105 KB without the layer (the layer costs ~2.5× and buys a
  second evaluator). The full custom VM stays deferred to the R2-04 slot, after live telemetry.

## What would change the outcome

Only two routes are visible, and both are real work rather than a re-roll:

1. **Drop the JSO-light pass on e3** and ship `terser → js-confuser(rgf)` alone (one obfuscator instead
   of two). That removes the interaction that breaks it — but it also removes the light pass's own
   protections from that piece, and it needs its own gate run (matrix/tiers/carrier-flip/fullwire).
2. **Teach the JSO stage to keep `let`/`const` bindings out of catch-path calls** (i.e. fix the pass
   rather than the pipeline) — a real change to the light-pass configuration, with its own verification.

Neither is in this pass. Recommend deciding after the live test, when we know whether the e3 piece is
being looked at at all.
